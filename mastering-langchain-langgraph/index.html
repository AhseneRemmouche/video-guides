Mastering LangChain & LangGraph: a hands-on companion

Build an assistant for a public library while you read Mastering LangChain and LangGraph. Each lab is one small Python program on the current LangChain 1.x and LangGraph 1.x, run on your own computer with free local models. Every lab was run for this guide, and its real output is shown, mistakes included.

Book: Mastering LangChain and LangGraph, 1st edition · ISBN 979-8-8688-2945-1
What this guide is, and what it is not. It is an independent study companion, written from scratch. It is not a summary of the book and does not reproduce its text, examples or code. Each lab and concept chapter tells you which book chapter to read with it. The library, its documents, its catalog and all the code are original.

How to use it

  1. Read a book chapter, then do its lab: labs 01–16 follow chapters 3–18 in order, and each lab page names its chapter. Or start with the lab if you learn best by doing.
  2. Read the concept chapter for that part of the book when you want the bigger picture: why things work the way they do, and what else exists.
  3. Change something (a prompt, a chunk size, a model) and run the lab again. The labs print enough to see what changed.

The project: Harbor City Library

All 16 labs work on one project, harbor-library-ai: an assistant for Harbor City Library, an invented public library with three branches. Its knowledge is six short policy documents (membership, loans, late fees, branches, digital services, study rooms), a catalog of 12 items and three member accounts. Because everything is made up, you always know the right answer and can catch the model when it is wrong. It is wrong more often than you might expect, and the labs show where.
