Which path should you take?

The AI workflow, Remotion, and Three.js each suit different needs. A quick guide to picking one - or combining them.

This guide offers three ways to make the same kind of animated explainer video: the no-code AI workflow from the video, a coded 2D version with Remotion, and a 3D version with Three.js. They all reach a finished video; they just differ in how much control you have and how much you need to learn. This page helps you pick where to start.

You do not have to choose only one. A common path is to try the AI workflow first to feel the idea, then move to code when you want results you can repeat and fine-tune. But if you are short on time, read the quick comparison below and jump straight to the part that fits.

The three paths at a glance

Each path trades some things for others. The AI workflow is the fastest to a first result but gives you the least control. Remotion and Three.js take setup and some coding, but you can change one detail and re-render exactly, as many times as you like.

How they compare

Here are the five things most people weigh up, side by side.

Why reproducibility is the big divide

The sharpest difference is reproducibility. In the AI workflow, Claude is non-deterministic: the same prompt can give a different video each time, and you cannot nudge one small thing without risking the rest changing. In code, the drawing is a fixed function of time, so the same source always produces the same frames. If you want to tweak one colour or timing and keep everything else identical, code is the way.

The 2D versus 3D question

The AI workflow and plain Remotion both produce flat, 2D motion graphics - shapes and text moving on a flat canvas, which is what most explainer videos are. Three.js adds a real third dimension: objects have depth, a camera can orbit them, and light falls across surfaces. That extra realism costs extra learning. If your idea reads clearly in 2D, you may not need 3D at all.

A quick way to decide

If you are unsure, this short decision path covers most people.

One more tip: these are not walls between you and the others. You can start with the AI workflow, learn how a video is timed to a voice-over, and carry that understanding straight into the Remotion and Three.js parts, which reuse the same airplane example.
