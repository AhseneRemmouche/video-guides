Step 10 of 15 · Extension - beyond the video

Add the voice-over and sync it to the narration

Dropping in the voice-over with the Audio component and driving the animation from a per-line timeline so picture and sound line up.

Extension - our own code, rendered to a real MP4

The picture moves, but it is silent. This step adds the voice-over and, just as importantly, explains how the picture and the sound end up locked together instead of slowly drifting apart. It stays an extension beyond the video: in Part 1 Claude produced the narration and the animation for you; here you wire the two together yourself.

About our voice-over file. In the real workflow from Part 1, the narration audio comes from Google AI Studio's text-to-speech. We could not call that service while building this guide, so our public/voiceover.mp3 is a local stand-in: the same nine lines read aloud by Windows SAPI (the built-in "Microsoft Zira" voice). It lets the whole example run end to end, offline, on any machine. When you make your own explainer, generate the voice in Google AI Studio exactly as Part 1 shows and drop your file in as public/voiceover.mp3; nothing else changes.

How the <Audio> line works

On Step 09 you saw the full component. Two things in it bring the sound in. They are lines 2 and 16 of Explainer2D.tsx:

import { AbsoluteFill, Audio, useCurrentFrame, useVideoConfig, staticFile } from "remotion";
// …
<Audio src={staticFile("voiceover.mp3")} />

The Audio component is Remotion's way of putting a sound into a composition. You give it a src and Remotion plays that audio on the composition's clock: during a render it mixes the sound into the MP4; during preview it plays it in time with the scrubbing playhead. You do not press play or manage the sound yourself.

The source comes from staticFile. Remotion serves anything in the project's public/ folder, and staticFile("voiceover.mp3") builds the correct URL to public/voiceover.mp3. This is why the voice-over file must live in public/: that folder is the one place Remotion looks for static assets you reference by name.

Why staticFile and not a plain path?

A hand-typed path like "./public/voiceover.mp3" works in some places and breaks in others, because the preview server and the renderer resolve files differently. staticFile hides that difference: it always returns a URL that works in both the studio and the render. Give it the name as it sits inside public/ and let Remotion do the rest.

Keeping picture and sound in sync

Here is the quiet trick that makes everything line up. The picture is driven by time: t = frame / fps. The sound is driven by the same clock, because <Audio> lives in the same composition and Remotion advances both together. The nine timeline windows were measured from this exact audio file, so when the narration reaches "the secret is the wings," the clock is inside scene 3's window and the wing is on screen. There is no separate audio timer to drift; both the drawing and the sound read one frame counter.

The window numbers live in timeline.json. It is the same nine cues as the timeline inside scenes2d.ts, kept as plain data with the narration text attached so you can see each line's start, end and length.

What does "in sync" really mean here?

Audio sync means the sound and the picture agree on time: the word you hear matches the thing you see. Films fall out of sync when the audio clock and the video clock run independently and slowly disagree. Remotion avoids that by giving both the same clock, the frame counter. Because our cue windows were measured from this very audio, picture and sound meet on every frame.

What you should see. Make sure public/voiceover.mp3 exists, then open Explainer2D in the studio and press play. You should hear the narration start at zero and the picture change in time with it: "the secret is the wings" as the wing appears, "faster across the top" as the airfoil's red line pulls ahead, and the closing line right as the plane flies off at 37.3 seconds.
Common mistakes.
  • Forgot public/voiceover.mp3. If the file is missing, staticFile resolves to a URL that 404s and the video renders silent (or the studio logs a failed audio load). Put an MP3 named exactly voiceover.mp3 in the public/ folder.
  • Wrong folder for the asset. The file must be in public/, not src/. staticFile only serves from public/.
  • Different audio length than the timeline. If you swap in your own voice-over, its length probably is not 37.295 seconds. Update durationSec and re-measure the nine cues, or the picture and sound will not line up.
Recap
  • Our voiceover.mp3 is a local Windows SAPI stand-in for Google AI Studio's text-to-speech; readers swap in their own file of the same name.
  • <Audio src={staticFile("voiceover.mp3")} /> plays the sound on the composition's clock; staticFile finds it in public/.
  • The picture uses t = frame / fps and the sound uses the same frame clock, so they cannot drift apart.
  • timeline.json holds the nine cues (start, end, length) measured from the audio; each line plays while its matching scene is shown.
  • Swap the audio and you must re-measure the cues and durationSec.
