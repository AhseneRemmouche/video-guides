Set up Claude: the model, the effort and Co-work mode

Starting a Claude project, picking Opus 5.5 at medium effort, and why Co-work mode is used.

▶ Watch this part (00:00:49)

Before you can ask Claude for anything, you choose a few settings that shape how it works: which model answers you, how hard it thinks, and which mode it works in. The video covers these choices right at the start (at 0:49). This step explains each one in plain terms so you know why the creator picks what he picks.

Open Claude in your browser at claude.ai and start a new chat or project. The rest of this step is about the three settings you pick there before typing your first prompt.

Setting 1: the model (Opus 5.5)

A model is the specific AI brain that answers you. Claude comes in families, and the strongest family is called Opus. The video uses Opus 5.5, because this task is creative and has several steps (write a script, then design a whole animated video), and the most capable model handles that best.

The creator also mentions (at 0:49) that Sonnet 5.5 had just been released. Sonnet is a lighter, faster tier that may be available without a paid plan. He raises it as a possible alternative if you cannot use Opus, while noting he has not tested the full workflow on it.

Setting 2: the effort (medium, on purpose)

Claude lets you choose how much effort it spends thinking before it answers. More effort can mean better answers on hard problems, but it also takes longer. It is tempting to always pick the highest setting, but the video makes a deliberate point here (at 0:49): he chooses medium.

His reasoning, from experience: medium effort already gave enough quality for these videos and was noticeably faster. On another video he tried a higher ("extra" or high) effort and the result actually came out worse and was much slower. So medium is not a compromise here - it was the better choice for this kind of work.

New to "effort"? Why a slower setting can be worse

Effort is a dial for how long the model reasons internally before replying. On a tricky maths or logic problem, more reasoning often helps. But on an open, creative job there is no single right answer, and extra reasoning can lead the model to over-think and drift. That is why the creator found a higher effort came out worse here. The lesson: match the effort to the task, and do not assume the maximum is always best.

Setting 3: Co-work mode (not plain Chat)

Claude offers more than one way to work. A plain chat is a back-and-forth conversation. The video instead selects Co-work mode (at 0:49), which is meant for working alongside Claude on a task over several steps - the kind of longer, produce-a-file job this workflow needs. Choosing the right mode here is what lets Claude go off, do the multi-step work, and hand back a finished video.

New to modes? Why not just chat normally

You could ask Claude things in a normal chat all day. A mode like Co-work changes how Claude approaches the request: instead of a quick reply, it settles in to carry out a longer task and deliver a result you can take away. For a job with several stages that ends in a downloadable file, that is the right fit. If you only ever use plain chat you may not get the produce-a-file behaviour this workflow relies on.

What it costs: the normal $20/month plan

You might expect a tool this powerful to need an expensive subscription. The video is clear (at 0:49) that everything on the Claude side runs on the ordinary $20 per month Claude plan - the same consumer subscription many people already have. The creator notes at the end of the video that the whole project cost him only that monthly plan. There is no separate per-video charge shown.

The settings, in order

  1. Open claude.ai in your browser and sign in to your plan.
  2. Start a new chat or project for this video.
  3. Choose the model: pick Opus 5.5. If it is not available to you, note Sonnet 5.5 as a lighter alternative.
  4. Set the effort to medium - not the highest setting.
  5. Switch the mode to Co-work rather than plain Chat.
  6. Leave it there for now. The next step is writing Prompt 1.
